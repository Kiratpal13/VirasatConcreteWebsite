Concrete Repair vs Replacement: Calgary Cost Guide

Published: June 14, 20265 min read

If you have cracks, scaling, or sinking sections, the biggest question is whether to repair now or replace the slab. In Calgary, freeze-thaw stress can turn small defects into major issues quickly.

How to Decide: Start With Severity

Not every crack means replacement. The right decision depends on crack width, movement, base condition, and how much of the slab is affected.

  • Minor: Hairline cracks, shallow surface wear
  • Moderate: Repeated cracking, larger spalled areas
  • Severe: Heaving, settlement, broken slab sections

Choose Repair When

  • Damage is limited to surface areas
  • Cracks are narrow and not moving
  • Base remains stable

Repairs are usually best for extending lifespan while keeping costs controlled, especially when the slab is still structurally sound.

Choose Replacement When

  • Cracks are wide or recurring
  • Sections are heaving or settling
  • More than ~25% surface is deteriorated

Typical Cost Ranges (Calgary)

  • Minor crack/seal repairs: lower cost, short downtime
  • Partial resurfacing: moderate cost, faster than full replacement
  • Full replacement: highest upfront cost, best long-term reset

Hidden Costs to Consider

Focusing only on immediate price can be misleading. Consider future maintenance, expected lifespan, drainage corrections, and whether recurring repairs will exceed replacement value over time.

Best Time to Act

Early summer and early fall are ideal windows. If damage is visible now, don't wait until winter. Early action usually costs less than emergency replacement after freeze-thaw cycles accelerate deterioration.

Need an objective recommendation? A professional inspection can show whether repair is still cost-effective or replacement is the smarter long-term investment.
